Your 30-day plan

  1. 1
    Days 1–2 · Verify earnings

    Count paid jobs. Know what each leaves.

    Review the last 30 days of invoices and payments. Separate your own customers, subcontract work, and employer jobs. Check whether 66–67 describes actual jobs or what you could do.

    Also list tools, vehicle access, supplier terms, insurance, and startup cash. Compare glass and painting using all your working hours.

  2. 2
    Days 3–7 · Make local connections

    Ask about referrals and spare space.

    Start with the three Lexington shops in Customers, your existing contacts, and the property broker. Aim for eight relevant conversations and two written space proposals.

    These are activity targets. A willing host or affordable offer is not guaranteed.

  3. 3
    Weeks 2–4 · Try a small paid run

    Test one repeat business relationship.

    Seek a few paid trial jobs with clear pricing and payment terms. Track travel, hours, rework, payment delays, and parking conflicts. Group corridor trips only when the work covers the travel.

At day 30: commit to space only when actual earnings support the full cost, startup cash is available, and use, parking, access, and insurance are confirmed.

Before agreeing to a space

  • Full monthly and startup costRent, utilities, parking, shared charges, deposit, and required insurance. The $500 space budget does not cover every business expense.
  • Written parking and accessNumber of outside spaces, overnight stays, keys, opening hours, deliveries, security, and late pickups.

  • Customer vehicles coveredAsk a commercial insurance agent about vehicles in your care, outside overnight parking, shared premises, theft, weather, and exclusions.

Local setup & safe pickup

Lexington licensing and occupancy

The city lists an initial occupational-license application payment of $100 unless exempt, and a $25 commercial Certificate of Occupancy. Confirm the steps and inspections for the exact space and use.

A home-office approval should not be assumed to cover a detached garage or customer drop-offs.

Glass, calibration, and release time

Follow vehicle-specific and glass/adhesive manufacturer procedures. Arrange qualified calibration of driver-assistance cameras or sensors when the vehicle requires it.

Offer next-day pickup only when parts, installation, calibration, and safe release conditions allow it. An overnight stay alone does not establish safe drive-away time.

Make the first ask small.

I replace vehicle glass, including truck rope-ins. Who handles your glass work now, and where do delays or difficult jobs come up? If there is a fit, I would like to quote one job so we can see whether working together makes sense.

Three customer groups to test

Repair and collision shops: outsourced jobs and scheduling gaps.

Independent dealers: glass work to prepare vehicles for sale.

Truck owners and small fleets: suitable truck glass and rope-in work within your capabilities.

These are ideas to test, not measured demand or guaranteed higher-paying work.

Make word of mouth easier.

Use one accurate Google Business Profile, a clear phone number, and examples of completed work shared with permission. Ask satisfied customers for honest reviews.
