Make $500 a month repeatable.

The first goal is at least $500 a month safely available toward the garage after current job costs, existing business bills, essential living expenses, and money needed for taxes, debt payments, and a basic reserve. It is a starting planning target; your records still have to show whether it is achievable.

  1. Show the surplus. Use money collected and actual expenses. A practical test is to show the surplus for two full months; that is a planning suggestion, not proof future income is guaranteed.
  2. Price the whole move. Get rent, utilities, outside parking, added insurance, shared fees, deposit, setup, and working cash in writing.
  3. Raise the monthly target when needed. Aim for the greater of $500 or the full additional monthly cost, with room for slow weeks. Keep startup money separate.
  4. Confirm the space works. Written permission for the work and overnight cars, suitable access, and insurance come before a commitment.

Four routes that can reduce the cost.

These are arrangements to ask about, not offers that a shop has agreed to. Compare the full cost, travel, access, and responsibility for customer cars.

1. Scheduled shared bay

Cost lever: pay for agreed hours or days instead of exclusive space.

  1. Ask existing repair shops about unused time and outside parking.
  2. Get a written quote with access, utilities, storage, landlord permission, and insurance responsibilities.
  3. Try a short paid arrangement with clear exit terms, then compare the real earnings.

2. Partner shop, fee per job

Cost lever: a clear facility fee tied to work actually booked.

  1. Ask one shop whether it wants suitable overflow glass work handled at its site.
  2. Agree who quotes, buys parts, collects payment, handles calibration, and takes responsibility for vehicles.
  3. Record one trial job's costs, facility fee, total hours, and payment before expanding.

3. Work at an approved customer site

Cost lever: reduce fixed rent while using transport and tools you already have.

  1. Confirm which jobs and sites meet installation, weather, power, access, and safe-release requirements.
  2. Price travel, any extra equipment, and qualified calibration into the job.
  3. Book nearby work together and use a suitable indoor partner when conditions require it.

4. Build the fund through related work

Cost lever: delay the lease while painting, deck or fence work, or suitable paid shifts build surplus.

  1. Test one small offer in Opportunities using existing skills and tools.
  2. Record collected pay, costs, and every working hour.
  3. Set aside only what remains after current needs; revisit bay quotes when the monthly milestone and startup fund are supported.
